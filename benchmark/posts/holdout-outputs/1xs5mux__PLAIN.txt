I'm a business major and I'm stuck on the very first step: finding an idea. Every founder story I read seems to start from one of two places. Either the person is an engineer who sees a technical solution nobody else has thought of, or they spent 15 to 20 years in an industry and ran into a real problem so many times they had to fix it. I don't have either one. I don't have the engineering skills to spot what's possible, and I don't have the decades of experience to know which customer problems are real.

The obvious advice is to go talk to customers. But I keep wondering what I'd find that a consultant hasn't already found and framed better. If people get paid to study an industry's problems full time, what edge does a business student with a few interviews have?

I've been looking at healthcare, so I messaged a handful of consultants on LinkedIn and asked how they think about the space. Nobody has replied. I know cold outreach is a numbers game, but it's been discouraging.

So I'd like to hear from founders who don't have a technical degree and built something that worked. How did you land on your idea? Was it from a job, from a personal problem, from talking to people, or something else? And if you started where I am, with no particular insight, what did you actually do in the first few months?

Not promoting anything, just trying to figure out how to get started.
