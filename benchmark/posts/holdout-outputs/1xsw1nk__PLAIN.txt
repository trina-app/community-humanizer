Five weeks in, I built an MVP and have talked to about 20 potential clients. The conversations went better than I expected. Most were positive, interested, and happy to keep talking. But almost all of them hold back, and the reason is always some version of "come back when it's more mature."

Only 2 of the 20 agreed to a pilot. Here's the part I'm struggling with: both of them want just one small feature. It's something I built almost as an afterthought and never thought was valuable. It's maybe 10% of what I set out to build, and it's not the part I'm excited about.

So now I'm stuck between two options:

1. **Pivot fully** to that one feature, since it's the only thing anyone has actually said yes to.
2. **Keep building the whole product** and lead with that feature as the way in, hoping the rest earns its place later.

The pilots also mess with my business model. I'd planned around selling the full product, but nobody seems to want to buy the full thing. If the real demand is for one small feature, I'm not sure that supports the pricing or the company I had in mind.

Two pilots out of 20 is a tiny sample, and I know that. I also know "positive but not buying" is the polite form of no. But it's hard to let go of the original vision after five weeks of building toward it, even though I know that attachment isn't evidence of anything.

For those of you who've been through this: how did you cope with the gap between what you wanted to build and what people actually wanted to pay for? Did you pivot to the thing people asked for, or did that feature turn out to be a trap? And how do you tell the difference between "they want a more mature product" and "they don't really need this"?

Not promoting anything, just looking for perspective.
