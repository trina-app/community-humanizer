I'm early in my entrepreneurial path, and I've had a few ventures before this that didn't work out. Every time I bring up what I'm doing now, my family has something to say about it, my brother most of all. It's always "what about the failed ones?" and "at some point you have to quit if it's not working."

I get where it comes from, and yes, businesses can fail. But from what I've seen, the founders who made it were the ones who believed in themselves and kept going when it got ugly. I don't think you can get anywhere if you stop at the first few misses. You have to try.

I've thought about just saying "you're entitled to your opinion" and then not talking about it with them anymore. Arguing hasn't gotten me anywhere, and I'd rather save the energy for the work.

I also wonder if part of the reaction is that they've never really taken a risk themselves. They've never had to bet on themselves, so a no-risk mindset is what they know, and I honestly have a hard time relating to it.

How did you deal with family pushback at the start? Did you stay quiet and let results talk, or keep pushing back? Not promoting anything, just curious how others handled it.
