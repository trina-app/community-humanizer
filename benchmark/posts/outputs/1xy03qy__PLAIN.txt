I've been a senior dev at a company of under 100 people for a while now, and I've been quietly doing something I'm curious whether anyone else is doing too.

I paid for Claude Max out of my own pocket for over a year, even though the AI policy technically said not to. Recently the firm rolled out Max accounts to everyone, so I'm now covered, which is a bit funny given how long I'd been doing it on my own dime.

Over that time I've automated almost all of my work. These days my job is mostly supervising what Claude does: checking output, steering it, catching the occasional mistake. The actual hands-on work is a small fraction of what it used to be.

I did try to share this. I showed my colleagues how to automate the parts of their work they have access to. Nobody adopted it. Not one person. The result is that I finish in a week what others take about two months to do. My manager doesn't seem to grasp what that means, and as far as I can tell leadership above them isn't paying attention to it at all.

So here's my plan, and it's not a dramatic one: stay just a bit faster than everyone else, not so much that it raises questions, and use the spare time to learn things I never had room for. Painting is the first one on the list.

I'd call it AI-powered quiet quitting. I'm still doing my job and delivering, I'm just not converting all the efficiency into more output for the company.

Is anyone else doing something like this? And if so, how do you cope with it? I go back and forth between feeling like I've found a great setup and feeling a little weird about the whole thing, and I'd like to hear how others think about it.
