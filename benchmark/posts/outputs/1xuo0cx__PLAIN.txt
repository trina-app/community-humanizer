I'm early-stage and trying to really understand product-market fit. My working theory is that PMF comes from understanding customer problems deeply, better than anyone else does. But that's basically what consultants do all day: they talk to a ton of people in an industry, map the pain points, and figure out what's broken. So why don't consultants dominate as founders? You'd think they'd have a huge head start.

Here's what made me think about it. I heard about a healthcare startup whose founders said they interviewed only 10 dermatologists to map out workflow issues. Ten. A consultant might talk to hundreds of people in a single engagement. So either the edge is just volume of conversations rather than some special way of thinking, or the founders left out how many people they actually talked to. I honestly can't tell which.

Salespeople are in a similar spot. They see customer problems up close every day, and people say good sales reps need a consultant mindset to do it well. I'm seriously considering taking a consulting or sales job, not for the career, but purely to learn PMF firsthand by watching how problems get surfaced and what makes someone actually buy.

Two things I'd love input on:

1. If someone more experienced has probably already mapped the problems in a space, is it still worth doing the mapping yourself? Or is that duplicated effort, and you're better off building on what's known?
2. How does the consultant/sales route square with the fact that so many successful founders are young and never did either? Is it a useful shortcut, or a detour that feels productive but mostly delays actually building?

Not promoting anything, just trying to figure out the best way to learn this. If you've been on either side of this, I'd appreciate hearing what worked.
