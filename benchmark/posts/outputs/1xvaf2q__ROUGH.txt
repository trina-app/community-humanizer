I'm a CFO at a startup, and I've watched a lot of companies go through fundraising, some strong and some weak. Not promoting anything, just sharing what I keep noticing.

VCs mostly fund one of two things: proven demand or a proven team. What they want is evidence that there's a high chance of a big return. That evidence usually comes from demand you can generate and replicate at scale, a market whose appetite is growing, or founders who have already won before.

Uber in 2011 is the demand case. They had a pilot in San Francisco with real revenue and riders who kept coming back. And it looked replicable city by city, so the investor could picture it getting big.

Airbnb in 2009 is a bit different. The demand already existed on Craigslist and Couchsurfing, and it spiked during big events when hotels sold out. They didn't have to convince anyone people wanted it.

Then there's the team side. Beehiiv in 2021: the founders had built the growth engine at Morning Brew, which sold to Business Insider for $75M. Affirm in 2014 had Max Levchin, who'd already had PayPal and Slide exits.
