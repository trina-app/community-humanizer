Went to an AI keynote today and caught myself counting how many slides did the same thing. "X does Y. Z doesn't." Checking every line of code vs. checking every decision. Blaming the team vs. taking ownership. Over and over, slide after slide.

Then I started seeing it everywhere else. Ads, street banners, company websites, pitch decks. Once you hear it you can't stop.

My guess is it's Claude-style phrasing leaking into how people talk and write. Some of it probably isn't AI at all and just happens to fit the pattern, so I may be pattern-matching too hard. But there's also a gut feeling I get sometimes where I read something and think "Claude wrote that," even when I can't point to a specific construction.

I like the tool, I use it every day. What bugs me is the idea that it's quietly shaping how people write and, eventually, how they think. When I write something that actually matters to me, I want it to sound like me. My bet is that people who keep writing on their own will stand out and come across as more authentic.

Anyone else noticing this, or am I losing it? Feels a bit like putting on the sunglasses in They Live and seeing the same message on every billboard.
